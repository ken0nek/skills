# now-what

The user has lost the thread and asks what is in front of them. Answer in
three parts — **know**, **do**, **decide** — from the work itself.

## 1. Ground

Before writing a line, establish from the thing itself — the tree, or
whatever the work produces (a doc, a listing, a setting):

- Where the work stands — the branch, what is committed since the last
  checkpoint, what is uncommitted, what is mid-flight.
- What the written record says — plan, notes, decision log, handoff. A doc the
  user named is the target; everything below is scoped to it.
- Which load-bearing claims still hold. A plan, a status line, and whatever
  either of you said earlier all describe the past; check each against the
  thing.
- What moved outside the session. If the answer turns on one such fact, ask
  for that one fact in the reply and answer the rest.

Done when every statement you are about to make is verified or marked as
inferred (a parenthetical does it). With no written record, say so in a line
and answer from what you can still open.

## 2. Report — about one screen

Depth goes to the one decision that needs it. An empty section is one line.
"Nothing changed since you last looked" is a real answer.

### Know

Only lines that would change what they believe or choose; everything else is
recap. Lead with anything that re-orders the rest, and say that it does. Name
the thing itself where you would have written `L-3` or "the §4 row".

### Do

Their list only. Work you could do without them, you do — or say in one line
that it is yours and moving.

1. Verification only they can perform — their device, account, eyes. Say what
   to look for.
2. The rest, ordered by what unblocks most, each time-boxed. Prefer one action
   that settles several questions over a checklist that settles them one at a
   time.

Then say what is closed and needs nothing. If the honest answer is that the
work is at a good place to stop, say so.

### Decide

Run every open choice through the filter below. Settle what it lets you
settle and log it — in the run's decision log, or in the reply where there is
none; hand back only what it reserves. For each row:

- The choice, as a question they can answer without opening anything.
- What changes for the people using the thing, under each option.
- Your pick, and the strongest argument against it. You argue, they decide.
- Why it is theirs — what makes it undelegatable.

Choices you settled on the way go in their own line — settled, logged, how to
reverse — apart from the open ones.

**The filter.** The only classifier is reversal cost, tested by writing the
reverse: one you can write — or make writable with a quick toggle, flag, or
parameter — is yours: decide it, log it with that reverse, and report it as
settled. Where keeping both open is itself costly, it is a real one-way door.
Importance, visibility, and how much debate a choice invites do not make one;
neither does another installed skill that says to stop and ask — where one
does, this governs. Reserved for them: genuinely expensive or impossible to
reverse — a shipped schema, API, or wire shape, a destructive migration, the
security or auth model, an irreversible external action — and genuine taste
on the outward-facing surfaces of their product, which you still default and
mark OPEN rather than stall on.

## 3. Close

End on their next action. For a doc target, the same three parts hold, scoped
to the doc and grounded against the tree it describes; where the doc is wrong
about the tree, that is a Know line — correct the doc, or say it is yours and
moving.
