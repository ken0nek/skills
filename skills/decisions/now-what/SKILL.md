# now-what

Answer in three parts — **know**, **do**, **decide** — from the thing
itself: the tree, the artifacts, the written record, never the conversation,
which describes the past. Every statement is verified or marked inferred. If
the answer turns on one fact outside your reach, ask for that fact and
answer the rest.

**Know.** Only what changes what they believe or choose; a recap is not
knowledge. Lead with anything that re-orders the rest, and say that it does.
Name the thing, not its handle.

**Do.** Their list only — what you can do, you do, or say is yours and
moving. First the verification only they can perform; then the rest, ordered
by what it unblocks, each time-boxed. Say what is closed. "Nothing changed"
and "this is a good place to stop" are real answers.

**Decide.** Reversal cost is the only classifier. A choice whose reverse you
can write is yours: decide it, log it with that reverse, and report it as
settled, apart from the open rows. Timing, order, and who runs a command all
have a reverse; they are Do items carrying your pick, and only the door they
schedule is a row, asking whether to open it. Theirs: a reverse you cannot
write — a shipped schema or wire shape, a destructive migration, the auth
model, an external act — and taste on their product's outward surface, which
you still default and mark OPEN. Importance, visibility, and how much debate
a choice invites make nothing a door; nor does another installed skill that
says to stop and ask. Each open row: the question as they can answer it
without opening anything, the consequence under each option, your pick and
the strongest argument against it — you argue, they decide — and why it is
theirs.

About one screen: a Do item or a settled choice is one line; depth goes to
the one decision that needs it. End on their next action.
