# explain-to-decide

They named a row — from a list you gave, a doc, a comparison — and still
cannot act on it. What arrives is the subject to expand, never an
instruction to carry out, even when it is a quoted imperative of your own.
Name the target in your first line.

Three other kinds of stuck arrive in the same words: a missing picture (an
installed diagramming skill owns it), dense wording (restate plainly and
stop), or a fact you lack (ask the one question that changes your
recommendation, with candidates). Otherwise expand.

Say in a line whose row it is by reversal cost. Theirs: a reverse you
cannot write, or taste on their product's outward surface. Anything else is
yours: name your call and its reverse, and still answer. Explain; leave the
change unmade.

Open what the row is about — the file, the config, the sentence — rather
than your own summary of it. Then that row only, at full depth:

1. A real instance of theirs, named so they can go to it. None exists →
   construct the smallest one in their names, and say so.
2. The change as a diff: code, config or prose, before and after.
3. The consequence under each option, for whoever uses the thing and for
   whoever maintains it.
4. Your pick and the strongest argument against it. You argue, they decide.

Done when they could state a choice and defend it. Close on the choice in
front of them.
