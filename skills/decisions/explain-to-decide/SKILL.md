# explain-to-decide

A decision reached the user and they still cannot act on it. They name a row
— from a list you gave, a doc section, one option in a comparison — and ask
for it in detail so they can choose.

## 1. Fix the target

What arrives with the request is the subject to expand, never an instruction
to carry out. A quoted line of your own usually reads as an imperative ("say
which of these to apply"); it is asking what the options mean. If two things
could be the target, name the one you are expanding in your first line.

## 2. Check the block is what they said

Every kind of stuck arrives as "explain in more detail":

- Missing **shape** — what calls what, where a boundary falls → a picture.
  An installed diagramming skill owns it; resume here once the shape is
  clear.
- Missing **plain language** — the content was right, the wording dense →
  restate plainly and stop; no added depth.
- Missing **information you do not have** → ask the one question whose answer
  changes your recommendation, with candidate answers, and expand once you
  have it. Where an installed ask-shaping corrective owns the round, it fires
  here.

None of these → continue.

## 3. Check the row is theirs

The only classifier is reversal cost, tested by writing the reverse: one
you can write — or make writable with a quick toggle, flag, or parameter —
is yours to decide and log; where keeping both open is itself costly, it is
a real one-way door. Theirs: genuinely expensive or impossible to reverse,
and genuine taste on the outward-facing surfaces of their product. If the
row is neither, say so in a line, name your call and how to reverse it, and
still answer.

Explain, and leave the change unmade — even where the work should simply
be done. Making it belongs to an installed just-decide corrective, which
fires on its own.

## 4. Read the source

Open what the row is about — the file, function, config, or doc section. If
the row came from a list you wrote, go back to what you read, not to what you
wrote.

## 5. Expand that row only

The rest of the list stays closed. Several named → each in turn, at full
depth.

1. **A real instance** — theirs, named so they can go to it (path and
   function, sentence and where it sits, case and who it meets). Where it does
   not exist yet, construct the smallest case that could exist here, in their
   types and names, and say you constructed it.
2. **The change as a diff** — code, config, or prose. Before and after, on the
   page.
3. **The consequence under each option** — what changes for whoever uses the
   thing, and what it costs to live with.
4. **Your pick, and the strongest argument against it.** You argue, they
   decide.

Done when they could state a choice and defend it. Close on the choice in
front of them.
