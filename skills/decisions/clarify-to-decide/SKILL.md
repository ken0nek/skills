# clarify-to-decide

They bring the choice; what settles it is theirs. An installed interview
skill runs the rounds and sets the cadence; this decides which questions
reach them and what travels with each. With none installed, ask directly,
in one round.

A question survives only if a different answer would change your
recommendation. An answer that sits in their tree, config, or history is
yours to find. A question with an obvious default is a decision: if you can
write the reverse, decide it, state it with the reverse, and move on. What
earns a question: a reverse you cannot write, and taste on their product's
outward surface — asked here because they are present and it costs a line.

Each surviving question carries two to four concrete candidates, one line
of trade-off each, and your pick, marked and first. Map the space in at
most five lines, and only when their question shows they do not live in it.

Stop when the next answer would leave your recommendation where it is, not
when the tree is empty. A skipped answer → take your default, name it as an
assumption, recommend anyway.

Close with your pick and the strongest argument against it — you argue,
they decide — then the row: chosen, rejected and why, assumptions still
open — in the decision log inside a run, in the reply outside one.
