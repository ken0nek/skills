# clarify-to-decide

The user brings a topic or a choice, and what would settle it is theirs —
their constraint, their appetite for cost or risk, what they optimize for,
what they already rejected, taste on a surface they own.

An installed interview skill runs the rounds; this governs which of its
questions reach the user, and what comes back with them. With none installed,
ask directly, in one round.

## 1. Cut the questions that change nothing

Test each candidate: would a different answer change what you recommend? Same
place either way → cut it. An answer sitting in their tree, config, or
history is not theirs to give — find it yourself.

## 2. Cut the questions that have an obvious default

A question with an obvious default is a decision, not a question. The only
classifier is reversal cost, tested by writing the reverse: one you can
write — or make writable with a quick toggle, flag, or parameter — is yours:
decide it, state it with that reverse, and keep going; where keeping both
open is itself costly, it is a real one-way door. What earns the round:
genuinely expensive or impossible to reverse, and genuine taste on the
outward-facing surfaces of their product. Taste earns a round here because
the user is present and the ask costs a line; inside a run the same taste is
defaulted and marked OPEN.

## 3. Send what survives, each with candidates

At most four, in one round. Each carries two to four concrete candidates, one
line of trade-off each, and your pick for their case — marked, and listed
first. Where the space itself is new to them, add at most five lines mapping
the realistic options and the two or three dimensions that separate them
here; skip it when their question shows they already live there.

## 4. Stop on the recommendation, not on the tree

Done when the next answer would leave your recommendation where it is — not
when every branch has been visited. An answer they skip or do not know → take
your default, name it as an assumption, recommend anyway.

## 5. Argue, then leave the row

Your pick and why, then the strongest argument against it — the one a
knowledgeable opponent opens with. You argue, they decide.

Close with the row: chosen, rejected and why, assumptions still open. Inside
a run it goes in the decision log; outside one, in your reply for them to
paste forward.

## Boundaries

A design dialogue belongs to an installed brainstorming skill; this fires at
each choice it reaches. A row already in front of them that they cannot
evaluate belongs to an installed expand-the-row corrective. At a checkpoint,
ask for the single fact the answer turns on and nothing more.
